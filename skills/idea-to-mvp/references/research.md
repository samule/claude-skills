# Research (Phase 2)

Four tracks. Each starts from the hypothesis in `01-hypothesis.md`. Use web search and page fetching; cite the URL of every finding. If a source can't be read (Reddit and some review sites block automated readers), use what the search results show, try alternative sources, and note what you couldn't read.

## 1. Pre-mortem

Assume the startup has already failed 2 years from now. Find out why:

- **Dead startups in the space**: post-mortems, shutdowns, pivots (search "<category> startup shut down", "post-mortem", "why we failed", Failory, TechCrunch coverage). For each: what it tried and why it died.
- **Structural obstacles**: regulation, high acquisition cost, long sales cycles, platform dependency, a problem already solved well enough with Excel or a manual process.
- **Negative market signals**: a consolidating category, large players giving the feature away, no budget in the segment.

Close with the **3 most likely reasons for failure**, ranked, each with the evidence behind it.

## 2. Competitors

Map by tier:

- **Direct**: solve the same problem for the same segment.
- **Indirect**: solve the same problem another way (including Excel, consultants, manual processes).
- **Adjacent**: could easily add this feature (large platforms in the sector).
- **Potential acquirers**.

For each relevant competitor: what it does, pricing if public, segment, and traction signals (funding, reviews, size).

Then **steelman**: for the 2 or 3 strongest, write the best argument for why *they* win and this idea doesn't. Attack the proposed differentiators: are they defensible, or copyable in a sprint?

## 3. Voice of the customer

Find what real users say about the problem and the competitors:

- **Reddit** (`site:reddit.com <problem>`, `site:reddit.com <competitor> alternative`), Hacker News, industry forums, segment communities and groups.
- **Reviews**: G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, App Store / Google Play, Product Hunt. Prioritize 2- and 3-star reviews: they hold the most concrete complaints.

Deliver:
- **Top unresolved complaints** about current solutions, with rough frequency and short quotes with their URL.
- **The exact language** users reach for when describing the problem (useful for interviews and messaging).
- If the hypothesis addresses any of those complaints, say so: that's evidence of fit. If it addresses none, say that too.

## 4. Market and trends

- Rough **TAM / SAM / SOM** from public data, with every assumption explicit and sourced. Flag the most fragile assumptions.
- Market state: expanding, consolidating, or mature.
- **Buyer map**: who holds the budget, who influences, who uses it, and whether they are the same person.
- **3 trends** (regulatory, technological, or demographic) likely to affect the market in the next 2 years; each marked as a tailwind or headwind for this hypothesis.
- **Analogous markets**: where a similar problem was solved, and what worked.
